Where the world's islands are

Count every island in a detailed open map of the world's shorelines and you get {{counts.n|int}}. Over half are rocks and islets under a tenth of a square kilometre; {{counts.ge1|int}} are a square kilometre or more. Who has them, how big are they, and how many of us live on one?

How many islands are there in the world? It sounds like a question with an answer, and it is not. Sweden claims more than a quarter of a million, Norway and Finland boast of theirs, and Indonesia has spent years recounting its own. The number depends on what you count as an island and, above all, on how closely you look: every map smooths the coast at some scale, and each step closer adds rocks, skerries and sandbanks that the step before could not see.

So I picked one map and counted everything on it. GSHHG, the Global Self-consistent, Hierarchical, High-resolution Geography database, is a free shoreline of the whole world, drawn in one consistent way and detailed down to rocks of a few hundred square metres. It draws {{counts.n|int}} islands: {{counts.sea|int}} in the sea, {{counts.lake|int}} in lakes and rivers and {{counts.ant|int}} around Antarctica. I gave each one a size, a country and a population from WorldPop's 2025 estimates, which put people on a one-kilometre grid. Zoom in anywhere, search a country, and click any island.

    02 · How manyOver half of them are rocks

    Islands are overwhelmingly small. {{counts.under01|int}} of the {{counts.n|int}} ({{counts.under01_pct|pc}}) are under 0.1 km², smaller than fourteen football pitches, and together they hold less than a tenth of one per cent of the island land. {{counts.ge1|int}} islands are 1 km² or more, {{counts.ge10|int}} are 10 km² or more, {{counts.ge1000|int}} are 1,000 km² or more, and just {{counts.ge100000|int}} are bigger than 100,000 km², from Greenland down to Iceland. The biggest ten hold {{counts.top10_area_pct|pc}} of all island land, and Greenland alone {{counts.greenland_pct_isl_area|pc}}.

    The count of small islands is a property of the map more than of the world. GSHHG comes in five resolutions, each simplified from the full one. The crudest draws {{res.1.n|int}} islands, the low {{res.2.n|int}}, the intermediate {{res.3.n|int}}, the high {{res.4.n|int}} and the full {{res.5.n|int}}. Natural Earth's detailed 1:10 million map, the base of countless world maps, has {{res.0.n|int}}. But look only at islands of 1 km² or more and the count settles: {{res.3.ge1|int}}, {{res.4.ge1|int}} and {{res.5.ge1|int}} in the three finest GSHHG versions. Below a square kilometre, any total is mostly a statement about the ruler you used.

    03 · Who has themCanada, then everyone else

    Canada has the most islands on every measure: {{top_countries.all.0.n|int}} of all sizes and {{top_countries.ge1.0.ge1|int}} of 1 km² or more, from Baffin, Victoria and Ellesmere in the Arctic to the lakes and coasts of the south. Counting every rock, Greenland is next with {{top_countries.all.1.n|int}}, then Finland with {{top_countries.all.2.n|int}} (nearly all along its coast and in the Archipelago Sea: the shoreline has few of the islands in Finland's lakes), the United States, Norway and Sweden. Counting islands of 1 km² or more, the order changes: the United States ({{top_countries.ge1.1.ge1|int}}), Indonesia ({{top_countries.ge1.2.ge1|int}}), Russia ({{top_countries.ge1.3.ge1|int}}) and Chile ({{top_countries.ge1.4.ge1|int}}). The Nordic skerries are many but tiny; the Indonesian and Chilean islands are fewer but bigger.

    04 · The biggestGreenland, New Guinea, Borneo

    Leaving out the continents (Australia is the smallest of them, at more than three times Greenland's size), the largest island is Greenland at {{largest.0.area|int}} km², then New Guinea ({{largest.1.area|int}} km²), Borneo, Madagascar, Baffin and Sumatra. Some are shared: New Guinea between Indonesia and Papua New Guinea, Borneo between Indonesia, Malaysia and Brunei, and Ireland, Hispaniola, Timor, Tierra del Fuego and Cyprus each between two or more. Altogether islands cover {{counts.isl_area|m1}} million km², {{counts.isl_area_pct|f1}}% of the world's land.

    05 · PeopleOne person in ten lives on an island

    WorldPop's 2025 estimates put {{pop.world|m1}} million people on the grid. {{pop.isl|m1}} million of them, {{pop.pct|f1}}%, live on islands. Three islands account for {{pop.top3_pct_isl|pc}} of them: Java with {{pop.top.0.pop|m1}} million people, one in every {{java_one_in|int}} people on Earth; Honshu with {{pop.top.1.pop|m1}} million; and Great Britain with {{pop.top.2.pop|m1}} million. Luzon, Sumatra, Madagascar, Mindanao, Borneo, Hispaniola, Sri Lanka and Taiwan follow. The ten most populous islands hold {{pop.top10_pct_isl|pc}} of all island people.

    06 · Crowded and emptyXiamen and Devon Island

    Among islands of 100 km² or more, the most crowded is Xiamen Island in China, with {{dens.ge100.0.dens|int}} people per km², ahead of Singapore ({{dens.ge100.1.dens|int}}), Montreal (joined to Laval in this shoreline) and Staten Island. Of the islands over 1,000 km², Long Island in New York is the most crowded ({{dens.ge1000.0.dens|int}} per km²), then Java ({{dens.ge1000.1.dens|int}}). At the other end, {{empty_n_ge1000|int}} islands of 1,000 km² or more have nobody on them in WorldPop's grid. The largest is Devon Island in the Canadian Arctic, {{empty.0.area|int}} km² and genuinely uninhabited. The list also shows the limits of a population model: Spitsbergen, home to the town of Longyearbyen, also comes out empty, because the settlement layer behind WorldPop misses it.

    07 · Islands in lakes on islandsGlover Island

    GSHHG keeps track of what sits inside what: land, lakes on the land, islands in the lakes. {{nest.lakes_on_isl|int}} of its {{counts.lakes|int}} lakes are on islands, the largest a {{nest.biggest_lake.km2|int}} km² lake on Baffin Island (Amadjuak Lake; the bigger Nettilling Lake is missing from the shoreline). {{nest.n|int}} islands sit in a lake on an island. The largest is Glover Island, {{nest.top.0.area|int}} km² of forest in Grand Lake on Newfoundland. Then come Talim Island in Laguna de Bay and Volcano Island in Taal Lake, both on Luzon, and Isla Cabritos in Lake Enriquillo on Hispaniola. Volcano Island has a crater lake with its own islet, Vulcan Point, but the shoreline stops one level short of that.

    Samosir, in Lake Toba on Sumatra, is often called the largest island within an island. In GSHHG it is not an island at all: a narrow isthmus, cut only by a canal, joins it to the lake shore. The same kind of judgement decides much bigger questions. Manhattan and Mumbai's Salsette Island are part of the mainland in this shoreline, so their people count as continental; the Island of Montreal and Laval are drawn as one.

    {{pop.isl|m0}} million people, {{pop.pct|f1}}% of humanity, live on islands. Java alone holds more than all of North America's islands put together.

    08 · How I did thisMethods and caveats

    Islands. GSHHG 2.3 at full resolution (Wessel and Smith), as packaged in the basemap-data-hires Python package. Its polygons are ranked by level: 1 is land, 2 a lake, 3 an island in a lake. Every level-1 polygon except the five continents (Eurasia, Africa, North America, South America and Australia, each over 5 million km²) is a sea island; every level-3 polygon is an island in a lake or river; Antarctica is kept separately in GSHHG, and its {{counts.ant|int}} islands are counted too. Pieces cut at the date line were joined. Areas are GSHHG's own, within the coastline, lakes included. The smallest island in the data is {{counts.min_m2|int}} m².

    Countries and regions. Each island takes the Natural Earth country under it, or the nearest one; for islands over 1,000 km² I measured the share in each country and gave the island to the largest. Regions follow Natural Earth's continents, with Hawaii, Easter Island and New Guinea in Oceania, the French and Dutch Caribbean in the Caribbean, and Asian Russia in Asia. Names come from Natural Earth's physical labels, and I named by hand the larger islands it leaves out, checking each by position and size.

    People. WorldPop Global 2 (release 2024B), the constrained 2025 estimate at 30 arc seconds, about 1 km, as re-tiled on GitHub by the world-population-3d project ({{meta.n_cells|int}} populated cells, those with 3 or more people). Each cell goes to the land polygon under its centre; coastal cells whose centre falls in the sea go to an island that touches them or to the nearest land within two cells. Only {{pop.unplaced|m1}} million people could not be placed. A 1 km cell cannot split a city across a narrow channel, so small islands next to big cities are rough.

    What this cannot say. Counts below 1 km² depend on the map's resolution. GSHHG is built from older shoreline data: it misses reclaimed land, some small lake and river islands, and Nettilling Lake, and it joins some islands to their neighbours or the mainland (Manhattan, Salsette, Samosir, Montreal with Laval) while it splits others (the Brodeur Peninsula is cut off from Baffin Island). WorldPop is a model: it places people by satellite-detected buildings, so it can miss small remote settlements. Natural Earth's borders are de facto, not a view on any dispute.
